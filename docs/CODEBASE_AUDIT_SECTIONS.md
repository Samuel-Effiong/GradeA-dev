test-audit pass (see `docs/CODE_REVIEW_STANDARDS.md` for the checklist, and

Each section lists: what it covers, why it matters, and what "done" looks

### Refund of a purchase: the business rule (decided 2026-09-09)

The question was open, not decided: `charge.refunded` recorded the money
and left the credits alone. Measured on a real handler run — a fully
refunded 500-credit overage purchase left the transaction `REFUNDED` and
all 500 credits spendable. That was an accident of the implementation, not
a policy.

**The rule now is: a refund reclaims what the payment bought, in
proportion to the money returned.** Absorbing the loss deliberately is
still available, but has to be asked for — set `retain_credits` in the
Stripe refund (or charge) metadata — and is then recorded as a decision on
the `PaymentRefund` row rather than happening silently.

The governing invariant: *financial records and customer entitlement must
never silently diverge from the final payment state.* Full rules, and the
reasoning for each, are in the `billing/payment_refunds.py` module
docstring, which is the authority; `billing/credit_reversal.py` documents
the shared accounting that stops a refund and a chargeback on the same
payment from reversing the same credits twice.

Two related defects were found and fixed at the same time: a chargeback on
an overage purchase reclaimed nothing (the reversal only understood
subscription payments), and the reversal engine's original lock order
deadlocked against a customer spending while a refund was applied — found
by a real-thread concurrency test, not by review.

Refunds of **subscription** invoices remain money-only and are explicitly
out of scope; the allocation is entangled with plan changes and
prorations, and inventing a rule there would be guesswork. Tracked
separately.

**Why:** this is the highest-blast-radius app — real money, Stripe webhooks,
and the append-only ledger just hardened in `e2dc83b`. Errors here are
financial, not cosmetic.

**Done when:** every write path to `CreditLedger` / `CreditUsageLog` is
confirmed append-only; webhook signature verification and timeout sync
(`scripts/check_gunicorn_timeout_sync.py`) are re-verified, not assumed;
`billing/tests/` and `BILLING_SERVICE_REVIEW.md` findings are cross-checked
against current code (that doc may be stale — verify, don't just cite it).

**Covered:** `grading/models.py`, `views.py`, `admin.py`, `tests.py`,

app registered zero models; the database held zero `ContentType`,

repo-wide sweep found only the `INSTALLED_APPS` entry and the
`.coveragerc` line outside the app's own directory. So there is no data
migration to write and nothing for a deploy to clean up.

**Done when:** `grading/` is gone from the tree, `INSTALLED_APPS` and
`.coveragerc`; `manage.py check` and `makemigrations --check` are clean;
the regression suite passes. All confirmed in the §6 pass — see the task
